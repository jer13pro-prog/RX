# RX Recruiting — Google Business Profile Setup Pack

Everything below is copy-paste ready. You do the account/verification steps (Google
requires the real owner to do those); the content is written and waiting.

Start at **google.com/business** → "Manage now" → sign in with the Google account you
want to own this listing long-term. **Use a real business-ish account you'll keep**,
not a throwaway — moving ownership later is a hassle.

---

## 1. Business name

```
RX Recruiting
```

**Do not** add keywords like "Running Coach Bakersfield" to the name. Google's naming
rules only allow your real-world business name, and keyword-stuffed names are the #1
cause of listings getting suspended. The keywords belong in the category and
description fields instead, where they actually count.

---

## 2. Category

Type into the category field and pick the closest match Google offers you — the exact
list shifts by region, so choose from what appears rather than expecting these verbatim.

**Primary category — try in this order, take the first that appears:**
1. `Personal Trainer`
2. `Sports School`
3. `Coaching Center`

Personal Trainer is the one that most reliably surfaces for "running coach near me"
type searches, even though the label is imperfect for what you do.

**Secondary categories (add 2–3):**
- `Educational Consultant` ← this is the one that catches the recruiting side
- `Sports School`
- `Athletic Club` or `Sports Club`

---

## 3. Location — set this up as a service-area business

When Google asks *"Do you want to add a location customers can visit?"* → **No.**

You coach athletes and run recruiting remotely and around the Valley. Saying no hides
your home address from the public listing, which you want.

**Service areas to enter:**
```
Bakersfield, CA
Kern County, CA
Fresno, CA
Visalia, CA
Delano, CA
Tulare, CA
```

You can list up to 20 areas. Don't stretch it to the whole state — Google weights
relevance by distance and an overstuffed service area dilutes you in Bakersfield,
which is where you actually want to rank.

## 7. Photos — this matters more than people expect

Profiles with photos get meaningfully more clicks than ones without. Upload before you
even finish verification:

- **Logo:** the RX mark
- **Cover photo:** you coaching, or a race shot
- **At least 5 more:** race photos from FPU, workout/track shots, you with athletes
  (get permission for any photo with a minor in it), a screenshot-free headshot

Real photos of you beat stock imagery every time here — parents are hiring a person.

---

## 8. Verification

For a service-area business Google will most likely ask for **video verification**:
a short unedited recording where you show evidence you run the business — equipment,
signage, branded materials, and yourself. It's less intimidating than it sounds.

Have ready: a stopwatch/coaching gear, anything branded RX, your phone showing the
website, and your ID. Turnaround is typically a few days.

## 9. Then, and only then — get the review link

Once verified: dashboard → **"Ask for reviews"** → copy the short link
(`g.page/r/…/review`). Send it to me and I'll wire it into the site button that's
already built and waiting.

### An honest note about your review timeline

Your site currently omits testimonials on purpose until your first athletes commit —
which tells me you may be early. That's fine, but it means reviews build as clients
build. Two real things you can do now:

1. **Athletes you genuinely coached at Fresno Pacific** can honestly review you as a
   coach. That's real experience, not a fabricated review.
2. **Free College Match Report recipients count.** Anyone who receives that report is a
   real customer of a real service. Ask each one for a review after you deliver it —
   this is the single fastest legitimate way to build early volume.

Never pay for reviews, never offer a discount in exchange for one, and never post one
yourself. Google detects the patterns, and a suspended listing costs you far more than
slow honest growth.

---

## 10. After launch — the part most people skip

- Reply to **every** review, positive or negative. Google weights owner engagement, and
  prospective clients read your replies as much as the reviews.
- Post updates every few weeks (a signing, a PR, a season recap). Profiles with recent
  posts hold rankings better than dormant ones.
- Re-check your hours and service areas each season.
